Scope note: Researched 23 Oct 2026. Every item carries a date where the source gave one. Items from before 2025 are marked **[OLDER]**. Reddit, Ekşi Sözlük and blog material is anecdotal and labelled that way. Vendor surveys (Go City, Expedia, Booking.com, eSIM sellers) are self-interested and labelled that way.

## 1. Traveler pain points in Turkey (research paralysis, tourist traps, price opacity, language, opening hours, inter-regional transport, last-minute changes, group decisions)

### Takeaway
The pain points with the strongest evidence for Turkey are about money and honesty more than logistics:
- **Price opacity and overcharging.** This covers taxis, restaurant bills, the euro-priced foreigner tickets at state monuments, and a domestic perception that Turkish resorts are now as expensive as going abroad. Turkish regulators reacted hard in 2026: they banned servis/kuver/masa fees (Jan 2026), and a draft bill would make cheating tourists an economic crime (June 2026).
- **Itinerary over-packing** across very long distances between regions, made worse by overnight buses and early-morning activities such as Cappadocia balloons.

Global surveys also show that money worries, stress at booking time, and coordinating a group's preferences are common to all trips.

### Cited Findings
**Price opacity / overcharging**
- **Galata, Oct 2025.** Four German tourists who came to watch a match were billed 59,000 TL at a fish restaurant a taxi driver steered them to. Ministry inspectors found about 30 items charged at prices different from the menu, plus service-charge irregularities, and fined the business 139,304 TL. The owner said the outrage was only a social-media perception of high prices. — [NTV](https://www.ntv.com.tr/ntvpara/galeri-galatada-turistlere-59-bin-lira-hesap-cikaran-restorana-kesilen-ceza-belli-oldu,Z0se2VNOhk-McZK0gnBZSg); [Cumhuriyet](https://www.cumhuriyet.com.tr/turkiye/mac-izlemeye-gelmislerdi-4-turist-59-bin-lira-hesap-odedi-o-isletmeye-139-bin-lira-ceza-2448060)
- **Etiler (La Scarpetta).** A tourist reported a 96,200 TL bill that staff later cut to 52,000 TL. Inspectors found the prices matched the menu but sanctioned 14 violations, mainly missing menu details and missing TL labels on drinks. Six products were referred for an unjustified-price-increase review. — [Ensonhaber](https://www.ensonhaber.com/istanbulda-yemek-yiyen-turiste-96-bin-lira-hesap-cikardilar-h1539823)
- **Ban on extra restaurant fees (Resmî Gazete, 30 Jan 2026).** Food and drink businesses (lokanta, kafe, restoran, pastane) may no longer charge servis ücreti, kuver ücreti, masa ücreti or similar.
  - Unrequested pre-order items (kuver/aperatif) cannot be charged.
  - No extra fee can be charged unless it appears on the price list.
  - Tips stay voluntary and cannot be added to the bill as a mandatory charge.
  - The maximum fine is reported as 1,439,000 TL. Amounts vary by source and were not checked against the Gazette text.
  - — [Alomaliye (30 Jan 2026)](https://www.alomaliye.com/2026/01/30/restoranlarda-servis-kuver-ve-masa-ucreti-yasaklandi/); [BloombergHT](https://www.bloomberght.com/restoranlarda-servis-ve-kuver-ucreti-alinmasi-yasaklandi-3767768); [GZT](https://www.gzt.com/gundem/ticaret-bakanligindan-tuketicilere-uyari-bu-ucretler-talep-edilemez-4245462)
- **Price-list display.** An earlier rule already required price lists at the entrance door and on tables. News coverage of the 2026 ban says the ministry also reminded businesses that a menu may not be offered only through a QR code. The QR detail was not verified in the primary text. — [GZT](https://www.gzt.com/gundem/ticaret-bakanligindan-tuketicilere-uyari-bu-ucretler-talep-edilemez-4245462); [NTV](https://www.ntv.com.tr/ntvpara/restoran-ve-kafelerde-bu-kurala-dikkat-sikayet-edebilirsiniz-1734893)
- **Draft tourist-fraud bill (TBMM justification text dated 22 June 2026).**
  - It lists the problems as excessive pricing, fraudulent invoicing, deliberately longer taxi routes and hidden commissions.
  - It covers taxis, restaurants and cafés, accommodation, currency exchange, tour guiding and souvenir shops.
  - It would treat this conduct as an economic crime rather than an administrative offence.
  - One article adds a duty to inform tourists of their rights in several languages.
  - Whether it has been enacted is **unconfirmed**. — [TBMM PDF](https://cdn.tbmm.gov.tr/KKBSPublicFile/D28/Y4/T2/WebOnergeMetni/d576bb34-01dc-43af-8b48-e036af45ab92.pdf)
- **2024 law.** A May 2024 amendment raised the fine for fahiş fiyat (excessive pricing) from 100k to 1M TL, and for stokçuluk (hoarding) from 1M to 12M TL. — [İşçi Haber](https://www.iscihaber.net/gundem/kanun-teklifi-kabul-edildi-fahis-fiyatlara-fahis-cezalar/112167) **[OLDER]**
- **Istanbul taxis.**
  - A reporter posing as a tourist near the Spice Bazaar in Eminönü found drivers turning down Turkish customers and proposing "traffic-avoiding" longer routes.
  - Police posing as Arab tourists were quoted €50 for short trips from Taksim to Nişantaşı or Şişli.
  - Both reports are undated in the search results. — [Hürriyet Daily News](https://hurriyetdailynews.com/istanbul-taxi-drivers-overcharging-scam-exposed-139002); [HDN](https://hurriyetdailynews.com/istanbul-taxi-scams-under-scrutiny-167021) **[date unverified, likely OLDER]**
- **Bar scam.** National Geographic's "Scam City" filmed a bar scam in Beyoğlu (1,500 TL for two beers). — [HDN](https://hurriyetdailynews.com/national-geographic-exposes-tourist-scam-in-turkey--35644) **[OLDER]**
- **Foreigner pricing at monuments.** Since January 2024, foreigners pay €25 for Hagia Sophia (upper gallery visit). Turkish worshippers enter the ground floor free. The Museum Pass Istanbul is not valid there. Children under 8 enter free with ID. Sources disagree on which gate tourists use. — [Travel Trade Journal](https://traveltradejournal.com/turkiyes-hagia-sophia-mosque-to-charge-25-euros-entry-fee-for-tourists/); [Orthodox Times](https://orthodoxtimes.com/new-entrance-fee-implemented-at-hagia-sophia-for-foreign-tourists/)
- **Domestic travelers and cost.**
  - Aydınlık (July 2026) reports a 163% year-on-year rise in Turkish visitors to the East Aegean Greek islands. Industry figures put this down to domestic and foreign holiday costs having nearly equalised. This rests on a single source and sector opinion. — [Aydınlık, 21 Jul 2026](https://www.aydinlik.com.tr/haber/hayat-pahaliligi-yerli-turisti-yurt-disina-mi-itiyor-dogu-ege-adalarina-giden-turk-turist-sayisi-ne-kadar-artti-yunan-turizm-geliri-silaha-mi-gidiyor-479933)
  - Visa-on-arrival for the islands is said to have emptied Bodrum and Çeşme. — [CNN Türk](https://www.cnnturk.com/yasam/galeri/kapida-vize-uygulamasi-sunan-yunan-adalari-dolup-tasiyor-yerli-turist-bakin-neden-kaciyor-3-kisi-64-90-euro-2134143)
  - Private beaches in Bodrum have minimum-spend limits starting at 2,000 TL (2026). — [Milliyet tag page](https://www.milliyet.com.tr/haberleri/bodrum-otelciler-dernegi)
- **Ekşi Sözlük (anecdotal).** Users complain about "fiyatların çıldırmış olması" (prices having gone crazy). — [Ekşi Sözlük](https://eksisozluk.com/entry/185263375)

**Inter-regional logistics and over-packing**
- An r/solotravel thread (Aug 2024) proposed 7 days covering Istanbul, Ephesus, Pamukkale and Cappadocia by overnight bus. Commenters called it "very very packed" and suggested spending the time in Istanbul instead. — [r/solotravel mirror](https://lr.eu.psf.lt/r/solotravel/comments/1ej10zb/turkey_7_day_itinerary) **[OLDER, anecdotal]**
- Balloon companies pick up from hotels at about 5:30–6:00 AM. Overnight buses arriving around 8 AM miss that window, so forum advice is to fly in the evening before. — [Fodor's](https://www.fodors.com/community/10529648-post8/) **[OLDER]**
- In one traveler's account, an overnight bus driver forgot the Cappadocia drop-off, and the traveler woke up in Ankara 4–5 hours past the stop. — [World Nomads journal](https://journals.worldnomads.com/shrummer16/story/34209/Turkey/Fairy-Chimneys-and-Tagalong-Pups) **[OLDER, anecdotal]**

**Language barrier**
- In EF EPI 2025 (published 19 Nov 2025), Türkiye ranks 71st with a score of 488. Speaking (438) and writing (441) are its weakest skills. — [EF EPI 2025 Türkiye fact sheet](https://www.ef.com/assetscdn/WIBIwq6RdJvcD9bc8RMd/cefcom-epi-site/fact-sheets/2025/ef-epi-fact-sheet-turkey-english.pdf)
- Language is also how bills get inflated: in the La Scarpetta case, the bill was written down once staff realised the customers did not read Turkish. — [Ensonhaber](https://www.ensonhaber.com/istanbulda-yemek-yiyen-turiste-96-bin-lira-hesap-cikardilar-h1539823)

**Connectivity (affects offline needs)**
- Foreign phones work on Turkish networks for about 120 days. After that the IMEI must be registered for a fee, or the phone is blocked. Only one phone can be registered every 3 years. Sources disagree on the fee, from about 3,000 to 45,614 TL, and on whether travel eSIMs are exempt. Several of these sources sell eSIMs. — [Turkey Travel Planner](https://turkeytravelplanner.com/details/communications/registering_mobile_phones_in_turkey.html); [Simology (vendor)](https://simology.io/blog/turkey-imei-registration-tourists-deadlines-fees-workarounds-2026)

**Generic planning stress (not Turkey-specific)**
- A Go City / Talker Research survey of 2,000 frequent US travelers (Dec 2024, vendor study) found:
  - 44% worry about overspending.
  - 51% choose destinations by price.
  - Planning (23%) and missing flights or travel (21%) are top worries.
  - Planning is the most time-consuming part of a vacation, about 18 hours. That figure comes from a truncated page.
  - — [Go City newsroom](https://newsroom.gocity.com/2024-12-12-Travelers-Underutilize-Technology-When-Trip-Planning,-Missing-Opportunities-to-Save-Time-and-Money-According-to-Go-City-Consumer-Survey) **[OLDER]**
- YouGov (Jan 2025) found 70% of US vacation bookers find booking stressful. Analysts say current tools do not address the pain points with the most impact. — [YouGov](https://business.yougov.com/content/51330-us-travel-stress-report-2025)
- Booking.com research (32,000 travelers, 2024) found the top group-trip pain points are agreeing on a destination, coordinating dates, and members wanting different things. — [Hospitality Investor](https://www.hospitalityinvestor.com/sponsored/travel-trends-bookingcom-talks-32000-global-travelers-about-their-2024-year-travel-and) **[OLDER]**

### Inferences
- A "fair price" layer could be a differentiator nobody else offers. Ideas:
  - Typical price ranges per category and city, refreshed often given inflation.
  - A "check your bill" helper covering the 2026 ban on servis/kuver/masa fees and the rule that a price list must be visible.
  - A "your rights" card in several languages that cites the regulation.
  - The foreigner/citizen ticket difference shown before arrival.
- The route engine should hard-limit unrealistic multi-region plans by checking:
  - door-to-door travel time,
  - overnight-bus fatigue,
  - balloon pickup times,
  - and suggesting flights between regions where they save a day.
- Budgeting needs a separate model for domestic users, who compare against Greek islands and foreign trips, and foreign users, who think in EUR/USD and see euro-priced monuments.

### Gaps
- No primary 2025–2026 Reddit or Tripadvisor-forum corpus on Turkey was retrieved. Reddit pages were not fetched directly, so pain-point frequencies are unquantified.
- No reliable source found on how often opening hours for Turkish museums or restaurants are wrong, or how often last-minute closures happen (e.g., museum Monday/Tuesday closures, Ramadan or bayram hours).
- No Turkey-specific survey from Skift or Phocuswright was found. Their reports are paywalled.
- Not checked: TÜİK or Kültür ve Turizm Bakanlığı satisfaction surveys of foreign visitors. These could quantify complaints.
- Whether the 2026 tourist-fraud bill has been enacted is unknown.

## 2. Fake and manipulated reviews: scale, Turkish rules (Ticaret Bakanlığı / Reklam Kurulu), EU DSA/Omnibus, detection techniques

### Takeaway
Fake reviews are a large-scale, industrialised problem:
- Tripadvisor caught about 8% of its 2024 submissions as fraudulent.
- Google blocked or removed about 292M reviews in 2025.

Turkey has moved toward a "verified purchase only" model. An amended Ticari Reklam ve Haksız Ticari Uygulamalar Yönetmeliği, in force from 1 Aug 2026, bans fake reviews outright. It requires verified reviewers, publication of reviews whether positive or negative, and objective ordering. Reklam Kurulu enforcement is frequent but mostly targets e-commerce and marketplaces.

For a free app that aggregates or shows third-party reviews, the Feb 2025 ruling matters directly. It found that a commercial site showing unverified Google reviews under "Tesis Yorumları" was an unfair commercial practice.

### Cited Findings
**Scale (global)**
- **Tripadvisor Transparency Report 2025 (covering 2024).**
  - About 2.7M fraudulent reviews were stopped, around 8% of 31.1M submissions. That is more than twice the 2022 share, partly because rules on incentivized reviews were tightened.
  - Review boosting by owners or affiliates was 54% of fraud.
  - About 360k removed reviews were tied to employee incentive programs.
  - 214k AI-generated reviews were removed.
  - 4.2M reviews (13.5%) were moderated.
  - — [Tripadvisor mediaroom](https://tripadvisor.mediaroom.com/2025-03-18-Tripadvisors-2025-Transparency-Report-reveals-strong-review-submissions-and-improved-fraud-detection?asPDF=1); [NBC](https://www.nbcsandiego.com/news/business/money-report/heres-how-many-fake-reviews-tripadvisor-found-on-its-website-in-2024/3833586/?os=vb)
- **Google Maps 2025.**
  - About 292M policy-violating reviews were blocked or removed, against more than 1B helpful reviews published. The 2024 figure was 240M.
  - 79M bad edits were blocked.
  - More than 782k accounts were restricted. Seroundtable says 783k.
  - More than 13M fake Business Profiles were removed.
  - When spam spikes, Google pauses new reviews on the profile, alerts the owner, and shows users a banner.
  - Gemini models now screen edits.
  - — [Google blog](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/maps/new-ways-were-protecting-businesses-on-maps/); [Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-maps-spam-fighting-2025-41176.html)
- **Google warning banners.** Google shows warnings on businesses caught with fake reviews. One 2024 report says these appeared only to UK users at first. Google does not disclose its detection methods. — [AlternativeTo (2024)](https://alternativeto.net/news/2024/9/google-maps-strengthens-fight-against-fake-reviews-with-global-measures-and-warnings) **[OLDER]**

**Turkey-specific manipulation evidence**
- **Reklam Kurulu, Sept 2023 decision.**
  - Businesses were sold paid "review management" services.
  - Negative reviews were hidden, and Google visibility was boosted in misleading ways.
  - One restaurant's glowing food reviews came from accounts that had only ordered small bottles of water.
  - — [Gün Hukuk PDF](https://gun.av.tr/media/2ohdxd3y/tuketici-degerlendirmelerinde-yakin-takip-donemi.pdf) **[OLDER]**
- **Trendyol Yemek.** When the platform started showing order contents next to reviews, it emerged that some restaurants had ordered water or ayran from themselves and then left 5-star reviews. — [Webtekno](https://www.webtekno.com/trendyol-sahte-yorum-restoran-puani-h130315.html) **[date not given]**
- **Ekşi Sözlük "sahte google maps yorumları" (anecdotal).** Users say many businesses buy reviews. In one user's sample, more than half of reviewers had only one review and used fake names and photos. Others describe people who were never customers leaving 1-star reviews. — [Ekşi entry](https://eksisozluk.com/entry/128799735)
- **Reklam Kurulu, 14 May 2026 (369th meeting).** 146 files were fined about 23M TL and 17 ads were blocked. The five-month total passed 122M TL. — [ticaret.gov.tr](https://ticaret.gov.tr/haberler/ticaret-bakanligi-369-sayili-toplantisinda-alinan-reklam-kurulu-kararlari)
- **Reklam Kurulu, 13 Aug 2026 (372nd meeting).**
  - 105 files reviewed, 87 violations, 32.68M TL in fines.
  - A company was fined 2,167,412 TL for not publishing a consumer review on its own website. The ministry said the review could have been published under a different rating category.
  - A restaurant was sanctioned for running many fake business names and addresses on food-ordering platforms.
  - 2026 totals: about 26,000 applications and 218.5M TL in fines. The article says both "first 7 months" and "first 8 months".
  - — [Ekonomist, 19 Aug 2026](https://www.ekonomist.com.tr/gundem/sahte-isletme-adi-engellenen-yorum-yaniltici-gorsel-ticaret-bakanligindan-yaniltici-reklamlara-milyonluk-ceza-77925)

**Turkish regulation**
- **Feb 2025 "Google reviews" ruling (345th meeting, 14 Feb 2025).**
  - The board found that a company had shown Google-sourced reviews under "Tesis Yorumları" without verification. It ruled that unverified reviews must not be shown on sites selling goods or services, since non-guests can post on Google. The company received a suspension order and a fine.
  - Many headlines said Google itself was banned or fined. **Google Türkiye denied this**: Google Reviews keep working in Türkiye, and the ruling concerns third-party commercial sites displaying unverified reviews.
  - — [Forbes Türkiye](https://www.forbes.com.tr/teknoloji/google-dan-reklam-kurulu-aciklamasi-yorumlar-ozelligine-yasak-getirilmedi); [CNBC-e](https://www.cnbce.com/teknoloji/google-turkiyeden-reklam-kurulu-kararina-iliskin-aciklama-h9818); [Dünya](https://www.dunya.com/gundem/ticaret-bakanligindan-google-hamlesi-tesislere-yonelik-yorum-sistemi-durduruldu-haberi-765613)
  - **Conflicting coverage, same meeting:** Hürriyet Daily News reports 205 files, 183 violations, 30.2M TL in fines, 37 access restrictions and 1 suspension. It frames the outcome as "only verified customers may review on Google", which conflicts with Google's statement. Türkiye Today reports 354 files and 183 violations. — [HDN](https://hurriyetdailynews.com/ministry-cracks-down-on-fake-google-reviews-206151); [Türkiye Today](https://www.turkiyetoday.com/business/turkish-trade-ministry-decides-to-suspend-google-reviews-123164)
- **Amended Ticari Reklam ve Haksız Ticari Uygulamalar Yönetmeliği, effective 1 Aug 2026.**
  - Fake consumer reviews are "kesin olarak yasaklandı" (definitively banned).
  - Reviews from platforms that cannot verify the purchase may not be published.
  - Where reviews are split under headings (product, service, delivery), all must be easy to reach in one place.
  - Influencers must label paid posts "reklam" or "tanıtım".
  - AI-generated characters in ads must be disclosed, and AI copies of real people making endorsements are banned.
  - Ad targeting based on profiling children's data is banned, and targeting criteria must be disclosed.
  - — [Takvim, 27 Jul 2026](https://www.takvim.com.tr/guncel/2026/07/27/aldatici-reklamlarla-mucadelede-yeni-donem)
- **Further rules attributed to Article 28/B of the same regulation.** Only purchasers may review. Reviews must be published whether positive or negative and stay online at least one year. Ordering must use objective criteria such as date, rating or seller. Reklam Kurulu has already blocked many sites that sell fake engagement. — [Dünya](https://www.dunya.com/ekonomi/ticaret-bakanligi-acikladi-sahte-yorumlara-yaptirim-uygulanacak-haberi-842114); [Ekonomist](https://www.ekonomist.com.tr/gundem/e-ticarette-sahte-yorum-ve-puanlara-karsi-yeni-donem-kurallara-uymayanlara-yaptirim-uygulanacak-79525) (Resmî Gazete text not verified directly; parts of this may describe draft stages)

**EU rules**
- **Omnibus Directive (EU) 2019/2161, applicable since 2022.** It added two items to the blacklist of unfair practices:
  - claiming reviews come from genuine customers without reasonable, proportionate verification;
  - submitting or commissioning fake reviews or endorsements.
  - It also covers selectively deleting negative reviews.
  - Fines can reach 4% of national turnover, or €2M where turnover cannot be calculated.
  - — [MakeInfluence](https://www.makeinfluence.com/en/academy/fake-and-manipulated-reviews-what-the-eu-omnibus-directive-bans); [RPC](https://www.rpclegal.com/thinking/consumer-brands-and-retail/retail-compass-autumn-2021/retail-compass-the-omnibus-directive-new-eu-consumer-rights-are-on-the-way/) **[OLDER framework, still in force]**
- **DSA and Italy.** The DSA has applied fully since 17 Feb 2024, and coordinated fake-review campaigns are said to fall within its scope (vendor blog, treat cautiously). The same blog reports that Italy's DDL PMI requires identifiable reviewers who used the product or service within a set time window, with fines up to €5M or 10% of turnover. — [Trustfull](https://trustfull.com/articles/fake-reviews-in-2026-what-the-new-eu-rules-mean-for-your-platform)

**Detection techniques (academic)**
- **App Store study.** 60k fake reviews from 43 interviewed fake-review providers were compared against 62M genuine ones. They differed in reviewer profile, rating distribution and frequency. A classifier built on these signals reached 91% recall and 0.98 AUC. — [Springer EMSE](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s10664-019-09706-9) **[OLDER]**
- **Amazon crowdsourced fraud (ICWSM 2017).** Malicious reviewers differ in rating distribution, review length and burstiness (reviews arriving in bursts). — [ICWSM](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICWSM/article/view/14953) **[OLDER]**
- **Unsupervised reviewer anomaly detection (JMIS 2019).** Builds distributions of reviewer features and combines them with a finite mixture model. — [JMIS](https://jmis-web.org/articles/1455) **[OLDER]**
- **Reviewer-level signals.** Average posting rate and share of positive ratings. — [arXiv 2003.00807](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.00807) **[OLDER]**
- **Caution:** benchmark detection accuracy is inflated by confounds such as product ownership and where the data came from. — [arXiv 2110.15130](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.15130) **[OLDER]**

### Inferences
- Legal and design implication for the planner: **do not show raw Google/Tripadvisor review text or star averages as "consumer reviews" inside a product that links to bookable services.** Under the Feb 2025 ruling and the 2026 regulation, that may count as displaying unverified reviews.
  - A non-commercial, ad-free app may fall outside "ticari reklam" scope. This needs Turkish legal advice.
  - Safer pattern: use aggregate signals as internal ranking inputs only, show the app's own curated rationale, and label clearly where any rating comes from.
- If the app collects its own user feedback, design it to the 2026 standard:
  - verification such as "I was here" by GPS or a receipt photo;
  - no suppression of negative reviews;
  - objective default ordering;
  - at least one-year retention;
  - clear labelling of any compensated contributors.
- Practical detection heuristics:
  - share of reviewers who have only ever written one review;
  - bursts of reviews over time;
  - J-shaped or bimodal rating distributions;
  - reviews whose content does not match the order (the water/ayran pattern);
  - a sudden shift in reviewer origin;
  - long runs of very short 5-star reviews.

### Gaps
- No quantified estimate of the fake-review share on Google Maps for Turkey specifically.
- Resmî Gazete text of the 1 Aug 2026 amendment (exact article numbers, including 28/B) not verified from the primary source.
- No 2025–2026 EU enforcement actions under the Omnibus review rules were found.
- Unclear whether Turkish review rules apply to a non-commercial, ad-free information app. Needs legal opinion.

## 3. Ranking and curation models that produce better picks (expert curation, wikis, social ranked lists, saved lists, signal blending, "where locals eat")

### Takeaway
High-trust curation in practice takes three forms:
- (a) **Paid or independent experts** with strict no-commission rules: Michelin's anonymous inspectors, Culinary Backstreets.
- (b) **Community wikis** with explicit anti-promotion policies (Wikivoyage's "Don't tout").
- (c) **Social apps based on comparison** (Beli), which force each user to compare places instead of handing out inflated stars.

For signal blending, the strongest evidence-based adjustments are:
- Bayesian or Wilson-style shrinkage, so places with few reviews don't dominate.
- Separating locals from tourists. Studies show tourists rate restaurants more generously, while locals judge harder and weight the food more.

### Cited Findings
- **Michelin Guide Türkiye 2026 (ceremony 4 Dec 2025).**
  - 54 new restaurants; about 170 in total.
  - Cappadocia added: 18 restaurants, its first star (Revithia, Ürgüp), and 5 Bib Gourmands including Babayan Evi (also a Green Star).
  - İzmir's first two-star restaurant.
  - Bodrum: a new star for Mezra Yalıkavak.
  - 39 Bib Gourmands in total (16 new).
  - Star count conflicts: 17 starred restaurants per one source, 15 one-star per Michelin's French press release.
  - Michelin announced a first nationwide Türkiye selection from 2026.
  - — [Michelin Guide](https://guide.michelin.com/gb/en/article/michelin-guide-ceremony/the-michelin-guide-turkiye-reveals-its-2026-restaurant-selection); [Michelin FR release](https://www.michelin.com/publications/produits-et-services/le-guide-michelin-turquie-selection-2026); [HDN](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/amp/turkiyes-culinary-revolution-night-that-redefined-michelin-map-216506)
- **Culinary Backstreets.**
  - Started in 2009 as the "Istanbul Eats" blog by Ansel Mullins and Yigal Schleifer.
  - Revenue comes from culinary walking tours, books, custom "Eatinerary" itineraries, and web and app work.
  - No sponsored posts or advertising; contributors are paid; it says it "would never accept a free lunch or any sort of commission".
  - Its journalism feeds new material into the walks.
  - No revenue figures are public.
  - — [CB Our Story](https://developments.culinarybackstreets.com/our-story); [TTG Media](https://www.ttgmedia.com/features/how-culinary-backstreets-is-shining-a-light-on-off-the-beaten-track-dining-experiences-16068)
- **Wikivoyage "Don't tout" guideline.**
  - Edits are reverted if they use first person ("our restaurant"), list one place under several headings, include more than one URL or referral codes, use superlatives, move a listing out of alphabetical order, or cite third-party ratings without justification.
  - Principle: "Describe, don't urge."
  - Owners must disclose their affiliation on their user page and give exact prices.
  - Paid editors may only suggest listings on the talk page.
  - Repeat violators can be blocked, and the business blacklisted.
  - — [Wikivoyage:Don't tout](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Wikivoyage:Don%27t_tout)
- **Beli (ranked-list social app).**
  - A new place is first sorted into "I liked it! / It was fine / I didn't like it".
  - It is then compared head-to-head with places already on the user's list until its position is fixed, with a "Too Tough" option to skip.
  - Outside analysts describe this as a binary insertion sort followed by mapping ranks onto a 0–10 score; this is not confirmed by Beli.
  - Personalised recommendations start after about 15 ranked restaurants.
  - — [HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/belis-binary-search-rating-system-explained); [Spoon University](https://spoonuniversity.com/school/brown/beli-the-new-app-personalizing-restaurant-reviews-and-recommendations/); [TODAY](https://www.today.com/today/amp/rcna217748)
- **Foursquare City Guide.** The app (the "tips" model) shut down on mobile on 15 Dec 2024 and on web on 28 Apr 2025. Tips, ratings and photos moved to Swarm venue pages, and data export remains available. — [Foursquare sunset page](https://foursquare.com/city-guide-sunset/); [Foursquare support](https://support.foursquare.com/hc/en-us/articles/17402902423708-City-Guide-Sunset)
- **Tourist bias in ratings.**
  - Xu, Zhang, Hong and Ye (*Information Systems Research*), about 71k reviews on a Chinese platform: tourists were at least 13.4% more likely than locals to give the higher rating, across price levels, genders and city sizes.
  - The mechanism: tourists shift attention from cooking, location and price to service, setting and emotion.
  - The authors note that a pooled average "may not be very helpful for either visitors or locals".
  - — [INFORMS release](https://www.informs.org/News-Room/INFORMS-Releases/News-Releases/Tourists-Give-Restaurants-Higher-Ratings-Than-Locals-New-Study-Finds); [working paper](https://mikezhang.com/files/TouristBias_working.pdf)
- **Locals on Tripadvisor.** In a study of more than 100k Washington DC reviews, locals were the harsher critics and food decided ratings for both groups. Tourists put more weight on location, payment, menu, atmosphere and how busy the place was. — [FH Wien](https://fh-wien.ac.at/en/news/stars-for-food-online-restaurant-evaluations-under-investigation); [IJCHM](https://emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/IJCHM-09-2019-0796/full/html)
- **Rating maths (Evan Miller).**
  - Raw average and positive-minus-negative both misrank items with few ratings.
  - For thumbs up/down, use the lower bound of the Wilson score interval.
  - "Bayesian Average Ratings" (2012) adds a prior and time decay.
  - A 2014 post extends this to K-star ratings.
  - Commentary describes the beta-prior posterior mean (a+k)/(a+b+n) and says Reddit adopted Miller's approach.
  - — [How Not To Sort By Average Rating](https://www.evanmiller.org/how-not-to-sort-by-average-rating.html); [Bayesian Average Ratings](https://evanmiller.org/bayesian-average-ratings.html); [Ranking Items With Star Ratings](https://www.evanmiller.org/ranking-items-with-star-ratings.html); [GreaterWrong commentary](https://www.greaterwrong.com/posts/SnSYB8CFpjESfRuCm/how-not-to-sort-by-a-complicated-frequentist-formula) **[OLDER but canonical]**

### Inferences
- **Proposed "local score" pipeline:**
  - (1) Classify reviewers as local or tourist using review language (Turkish vs other), reviewer history in the same city, and home-city signals.
  - (2) Compute a separate shrunk score for each group, Bayesian toward city and category priors, with time decay.
  - (3) Flag places where the tourist score is far above the local score as possible tourist traps.
  - (4) Prefer places where Turkish-language reviews talk about the food itself, such as named dishes.
- **Hybrid curation suited to a solo or small Turkish team:**
  - a small editorial seed list, e.g. per-city "esnaf lokantası" and çay bahçesi picks by vetted local contributors who disclose any conflicts;
  - Wikivoyage-style neutral-tone rules;
  - Beli-style comparison prompts after visits to build a user's personal ranking without star inflation;
  - Michelin/Bib Gourmand and Culinary Backstreets as external cross-checks (link out, do not copy).
- Foursquare City Guide's closure leaves a gap for "tips" (short, situational advice). Short dated tips with expiry, e.g. "closed Mondays", "ask for the Turkish menu", could fill it.

### Gaps
- Not researched in depth: The Infatuation's editorial model, Atlas Obscura's community/editor model, Corner and Mapstr saved-list mechanics. No sources retrieved.
- No study found that compares local and tourist ratings on Google Maps specifically, or in Turkey.
- Beli's actual algorithm is undisclosed.
- Wikivoyage's coverage and freshness for Turkey destinations was not measured.

## 4. Personalization: preference elicitation, explainability ("why this place"), group trips with mixed preferences

### Takeaway
Travelers are open to AI suggestions but trust established brands more than chatbots. Mood- and feeling-based discovery is a stated preference in 2026–2027 surveys.

For groups, the evidence says the aggregation strategy matters more than text explanations, and the best strategy depends on how far apart the members' preferences are. A dictator-style strategy (one person decides) hurts satisfaction.

### Cited Findings
- **Expedia "AI Trust Gap" (Apr 2026; 5,700+ adults in US, UK and India).**
  - 53% are comfortable with AI suggesting options.
  - 40% use or would use AI to build itineraries.
  - Only 8% in the US and UK rely on AI chatbots or agents for planning, versus 59% who use search engines.
  - Nearly 70% prefer booking with trusted travel brands over chatbots.
  - — [Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260414532485/en/) (vendor survey)
- **Booking.com.**
  - In the APAC survey (Jan 2026), 64% use AI tools while planning or during trips, and over half say it saves planning time. — [Skift, 27 Apr 2026](https://skift.com/2026/04/27/64-of-apac-travelers-use-ai-to-plan-trips-booking-com-laura-houldsworth-on-what-that-means/)
  - Travel Predictions 2026 (29,000+ respondents, 33 markets) themes travel as "unapologetically personal". — [TTG Asia, Nov 2025](https://www.ttgasia.com/2025/11/24/travel-in-2026-to-focus-on-individuality-booking-com-predicts/)
  - The 2027 predictions report that 81% would choose a destination by how they want to feel, and 76% would use a tool that recommends by mood. — [Hotel Management](https://www.hotelmanagement.net/business-strategy/bookingcom-predicts-travelers-will-go-script-2027) (vendor surveys)
- **Expedia Unpack '26.** About 24,000 travelers. 53% say what they see on screen influences their travel plans. — [Short Term Rentalz](https://shorttermrentalz.com/news/expedia-group-reveals-2026-travel-trends-in-new-unpack-report/)
- **Cold start (Beli).** Personalised recommendations unlock after about 15 places have been ranked by comparison. — [Spoon University](https://spoonuniversity.com/school/brown/beli-the-new-app-personalizing-restaurant-reviews-and-recommendations/)
- **Group tourism recommender (Najafian et al., HT '20).** All explainable aggregation strategies tested were rated well for satisfaction and perceived fairness, except a dictator-based strategy, which lowered both individual and group satisfaction. — [TU Delft "You do not decide for me!"](https://pure.tudelft.nl/portal/en/publications/you-do-not-decide-for-me(6cf52d4a-8ea2-48a8-8c55-c776c92ac62b).html) **[OLDER]**
- **Barile et al. (2023), two user studies (N=399 and N=288).** The best aggregation strategy depends on how varied the group's preferences are. Text explanations showed no benefit, contradicting earlier work. — [Springer UMUAI](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11257-023-09363-0) **[OLDER]**
- **2025 randomised study (n=271).** Compared additive, least-misery and approval-voting strategies with different explanation types, focusing on whether users understand the result when one member's tastes differ ("minority" scenarios). — [arXiv 2505.04273](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.04273)

### Inferences
- **Onboarding.** Keep onboarding fast with mood and intent prompts ("sakin / hareketli", budget band, food adventurousness, walking tolerance). Then learn from in-trip comparisons ("which did you prefer, X or Y?") rather than long questionnaires.
- **"Why this place".** Explanations should cite concrete, checkable signals: local-score vs tourist-score, price band, distance, and an editor's note. AI-sounding prose should not be the explanation.
- **Groups.**
  - Use each member's veto plus least misery for hard constraints (diet, mobility, budget).
  - Use additive or approval voting among acceptable options.
  - Show each member which choices honoured their preferences, since perceived fairness matters.
  - Avoid letting one organiser's preferences silently dominate.

### Gaps
- No Turkey-specific study of preference elicitation or group-trip behaviour.
- Swipe-style taste onboarding effectiveness in travel is not evidenced in sources retrieved.
- Nothing retrieved on how much visitors from different source markets (Germany, Russia, the UK, Gulf states, Iran) differ in what they prefer within Turkey.

## 5. Behavioural UX of in-trip assistance (morning briefings, nudges, notification fatigue, offline, one-handed use, voice)

### Takeaway
Travelers welcome alerts that are clearly useful: trip status, price changes. They opt out when there are too many or the alerts feel like marketing. Lock-screen silencing and Focus modes make losing notification permission one tap away, so a single daily briefing plus rare, high-value alerts is the defensible pattern.

Offline capability matters in Turkey because of patchy rural coverage and IMEI/eSIM rules for long stays. Strong 2025–2026 evidence specific to travel apps is thin.

### Cited Findings
- **Travel-app notifications.** One piece (undated, sample unknown) says 87% of travel-app users find push notifications useful; trip status updates, discounts and price changes are the most valued. Apps were deleted mainly for their size or because they were only used for one trip. — [Screenpilot](https://screenpilot.com/blog/how-we-use-phones-for-travel)
- **Why users opt out.** 25% of push opt-outs were due to too many notifications. Users switch off generic or marketing-driven alerts. — [Braze](https://www.braze.com/resources/articles/opt-out-of-push-notifications-why-users-do-it.md) (vendor, undated)
- **Notification volume.** Airship's 2025 Mobile Consumer Habits study is cited at 46–63 push notifications per user per day. A Localytics figure is cited: 52% of users who disable push eventually churn. Neither was verified against the original. — [ContextSDK](https://contextsdk.com/blogposts/avoiding-push-fatigue-common-user-turn-offs); [Appbot](https://appbot.co/blog/app-push-notifications-2026-best-practices/)
- **Platform controls.** iOS and Android both let users silence an app directly from the lock screen, and Focus modes are stricter than before (Jan 2026 practitioner guide). — [Appbot](https://appbot.co/blog/app-push-notifications-2026-best-practices/)
- **Connectivity.** Foreign phones must register their IMEI after about 120 days. Rules for eSIMs are disputed. — [Turkey Travel Planner](https://turkeytravelplanner.com/details/communications/registering_mobile_phones_in_turkey.html)
- **Offline maps.** Organic Maps' value is built on fully offline OSM maps. It reached its first million users in 2023. — [Organic Maps donate page](https://organicmaps.app/donate/)

### Inferences
- **Morning briefing.** One opt-in notification, at a time the user chooses, that bundles:
  - weather,
  - today's route and first departure time,
  - closures and holidays (e.g., museum closing days, bayram or Ramadan hours, Friday prayer timing at mosques),
  - a price-check reminder,
  - one "plan B".
- Everything else goes in the app. Interruptions only for time-critical events: transport delay, a closure affecting the next stop, weather risk to a balloon flight.
- **Offline pack per day.** Route, venue cards with Turkish names and addresses, phrase cards ("hesap lütfen", "fiyat listesi görebilir miyim?"), and a fair-price sheet. Keep the pack small, since app size drives deletions.
- **Ergonomics.** One-handed bottom-sheet design. Voice read-out of the briefing to help travelers who are walking or have low vision.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or travel-specific 2025–2026 study on morning-briefing formats, nudge effectiveness or voice use while traveling.
- Not found: Google/Think with Google travel research for 2025–2026 (the "app-nesia" study surfaced only as a headline and was not fetched).
- Turkish mobile coverage gaps in rural or archaeological sites (e.g., Nemrut, Lycian Way) not quantified.

## 6. Sustainability models for free, non-profit travel products (Wikimedia, OSMF, Organic Maps/CoMaps, Open Collective/GitHub Sponsors, EU/Turkish grants; API cost and burnout risks)

### Takeaway
Free open products survive on three patterns:
- (1) Large-scale small donations (Wikimedia, about $185M revenue in FY2024).
- (2) Membership plus corporate tiers (OSMF).
- (3) Founder money plus small grants and donations (Organic Maps).

Organic Maps shows the governance risk. Contributors alleged a lack of financial transparency, which led to the CoMaps fork in 2025; CoMaps now runs through Open Collective with a public ledger.

Biggest cost risk: proprietary APIs. Google Maps Platform replaced the $200 monthly credit with free usage thresholds per SKU from 1 Mar 2025.

Grant options:
- NLnet/NGI calls, open Sept–Nov 2026 with priority for EU and Horizon-associated countries. Türkiye's status was not verified.
- TÜBİTAK programmes exist but are oriented to R&D or companies.

### Cited Findings
- **Wikimedia Foundation.**
  - FY ending June 2024: about $185.4M revenue, $174.5M of it contributions.
  - Recurring donations are the largest channel ($36.2M per Statista) and a growing share.
  - The 2024–25 plan expected about 5% revenue growth and warned banner fundraising would not keep growing at past rates.
  - Wikivoyage is funded inside this pool; no per-project figure is published.
  - — [ProPublica 990](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/200049703); [WMF Financial Model 2024-25](https://meta.wikimedia.org/wiki/Wikimedia_Foundation_Annual_Plan/2024-2025/Financial_Model/en); [Fundraising 2024-25 report](https://meta.wikimedia.org/wiki/Fundraising/2024-25_Report); [Statista](https://statista.com/statistics/1311496/wikimedia-foundation-annual-donations-channel)
- **OpenStreetMap Foundation.**
  - Income comes mainly from membership fees and donations, with annual accounts filed at Companies House.
  - Members set the spending split: 60% hardware, 20% promotion, 20% legal.
  - 2023: about £373k from community and corporate partners combined; corporate memberships £201,074; small donations over £113k from about 3,089 gifts.
  - 2024: 8 new corporate members; Esri, Meta and Microsoft joined TomTom at Platinum.
  - Individual membership is £15 a year, free for active contributors.
  - £30k was budgeted for 2025 engineering microgrants.
  - — [OSMF Finances](https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Finances); [OSM blog Feb 2025](https://blog.openstreetmap.org/2025/02); [OSM wiki Donations](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Donations)
- **Organic Maps.**
  - Free and funded by donations via cards, Wise, LiberaPay, GitHub Sponsors, Patreon, OpenCollective, bank transfer (including TRY) and crypto. Recurring donations are preferred for stable income.
  - Legal entity: Organic Maps OÜ, Tallinn (Estonia).
  - — [Organic Maps donate](https://organicmaps.app/donate/)
- **Organic Maps governance crisis, 2025.**
  - A March/April 2025 open letter to the shareholders of Organic Maps OÜ asked for a non-profit structure, inclusive decision-making and financial transparency.
  - It alleged donations were used for personal expenses. This allegation is **unverified and one-sided**.
  - The community forked the project as CoMaps.
  - — [CoMaps open letter](https://comaps.preview.codeberg.page/website@714/news/2025-04-16/1/); [It's FOSS via CoMaps](https://www.comaps.app/news/2025-05-29/ITs-FOSS-NEWS--Organic-Maps-Forked-Over-Governance-Concerns-CoMaps-is-Born); [OSM Community forum](https://community.openstreetmap.org/t/organic-maps-open-letter/128851)
- **CoMaps (Jan 2026).**
  - Hosted under the Platform 6 fiscal host on Open Collective, which keeps donation management transparent.
  - Plans to move in 2026 to another fiscal host or its own non-profit association.
  - Lists financial transparency as a core principle.
  - — [CoMaps in 2026](https://www.comaps.app/news/2026-01-09/comaps-in-2026-planning-for-the-future/); [Open Collective](https://opencollective.com/comaps/contributions/867463)
- **NLnet / NGI Zero.**
  - Submissions were closed as of the latest page check; new calls open 3 Sept 2026 with deadline **3 Nov 2026**.
  - Software must be released under open licences.
  - An older NGI Zero Core call listing says there are no categorical exclusions, but EU residents and Horizon Europe associated countries get priority when proposals are equal.
  - — [NLnet propose](https://www.nlnet.nl/propose); [NLnet eligibility](https://nlnet.nl/core/eligibility/); [Cyprus funding portal (NGI0 Core 6th call)](https://www.fundingprogrammesportal.gov.cy/en/call/ngi-zero-core-6th-open-call-upgrade-the-open-internet-architecture/)
- **TÜBİTAK.**
  - 1001 (2026, 1st period): applications closed 2 Mar 2026; up to 3,000,000 TL; up to 36 months. This is academic research, so a university partner would be needed. — [Esenyurt Üniv. circular](https://www.esenyurt.edu.tr/uploads/2026/08/cxqmu3wcrjl2r-tubitak-1001.pdf)
  - 1501 (industry R&D, for companies): the 2026-2 call opened 20 Jul 2026; pre-registration deadline was 22 Oct 2026 (now passed). — [TÜBİTAK 1501](https://www.tubitak.gov.tr/tr/destekler/destek/sanayi/ulusal-destek-programlari/cagri-1501-sanayi-ar-ge-destek-programi-2026-yili-2-cagrisi-acildi)
- **Google Maps Platform pricing.**
  - From 1 Mar 2025 the $200 monthly credit was replaced by free monthly usage thresholds per SKU: 10,000 for Essentials, 5,000 for Pro, 1,000 for Enterprise (Map Tiles 100,000).
  - The legacy Places, Directions and Distance Matrix APIs were moved to legacy status.
  - — [Google Maps Platform March 2025 changes](https://developers.google.com/maps/billing-and-pricing/march-2025); [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2024/12/09/google-maps-platform-usage-limits/)

### Inferences
- **Architecture for zero or low marginal cost:**
  - OSM data with self-hosted or open tile and routing stacks;
  - offline-first data packs;
  - Wikidata, Wikivoyage and the app's own curation for content;
  - proprietary place APIs used only for occasional server-side refreshes, cached and within free thresholds.
  - Watch the attribution and licence terms (ODbL for OSM, CC BY-SA for Wikivoyage).
- **Governance should be set up before donations arrive:**
  - an Open Collective, or a Turkish dernek/vakıf, with a public ledger;
  - published costs;
  - a written no-paid-placement rule;
  - more than one maintainer.
  - This avoids the Organic Maps failure mode, and trust is the product's selling point.
- **Funding mix to explore:**
  - recurring micro-donations, Wikimedia-style, with transparent monthly cost banners;
  - NLnet/NGI calls if Türkiye's eligibility is confirmed;
  - university partnership for TÜBİTAK 1001-type research (e.g., fake-review detection for Turkish reviews);
  - municipal or Kalkınma Ajansı tourism-digitalisation calls (unverified).
- **Burnout risk.** OSM microgrants and NLnet fund features, not ongoing maintenance. Small, well-scoped grants fit better than one large dependence.

### Gaps
- Organic Maps grants and founder funding are unverified. A search summary mentioned an NLnet/NGI0 Entrust grant (search and fonts), a $1,000 FUTO micro-grant (Feb 2023), free hosting from Mythic Beasts and 44+ Technologies, and "most expenses funded by founders". The primary page was not retrieved, and the donate page omits all of this, so it is left out of the findings.
- Not confirmed: Türkiye's Horizon Europe association status for current NLnet calls, and whether Turkish individuals can receive NGI funds.
- No specific Kalkınma Ajansı (regional development agency) 2026 call for open-source or digital-tourism software was found. TÜBİTAK 1512 (BiGG) 2026 terms not retrieved; it is a startup or company programme and may not fit a non-profit.
- No 2025 OSMF accounts or current Wikivoyage-specific budget found.

## 7. Accessibility and inclusivity (disabled travelers, elderly, families with kids) in Turkey

### Takeaway
Istanbul's official transport claims full step-free access (elevators, ramps), but traveler reports describe patchy elevator coverage, crowded trams, little accessible bus information, and hard cobblestones around Sultanahmet and the Grand Bazaar.

Turkish hotel research finds wheelchair provisions are partial, and the needs of visually and hearing-impaired guests are often ignored. Staff attitude is also a barrier. Accurate, verified accessibility attributes would be a real gap for the planner to fill.

### Cited Findings
- **Metro Istanbul (official).** Stations have elevators, escalators and ramps from street to platform, with audio and visual announcements in elevators. — [Metro Istanbul accessibility](https://metro.istanbul/en/content/accessibility-services)
- **Wheelchair traveler guide (blogs, dates vary).**
  - Not all metro stations have elevators, and metro lines are far from the main sights.
  - The T1 tram is the most useful but often too crowded for a wheelchair.
  - No map of accessible bus routes was found.
  - Accessible taxis are the practical fallback.
  - The worst cobblestones are near the Grand Bazaar, with others around the Blue Mosque and Hagia Sophia.
  - Accessible toilets are scarce.
  - Call hotels ahead about elevators and roll-in showers.
  - The Basilica Cistern has an exit with a wheelchair lift.
  - — [Sage Traveling](https://www.sagetraveling.com/9-wheelchair-accessible-travel-tips-for-istanbul/); [Sage Traveling review](https://www.sagetraveling.com/Disabled-Access-in-Istanbul)
- **Istanbul Airport.** İETT buses at the airport are wheelchair accessible. HAVAIST accessible midibuses require a request at least 1 hour ahead. — [IGA accessible transportation](https://www.istairport.com/en/flights/airport-guides/iga-cares-accessibility/accessibility/accessible-transportation)
- **Hotels.** A study of 3-, 4- and 5-star hotels found them inadequate for disabled guests: some wheelchair adaptations, but visually and hearing-impaired guests' needs are largely ignored. — [HKÜ open access](https://openaccess.hku.edu.tr/items/302e52a3-bfad-43e1-93d3-448295137968/full)
- **Chain hotels in Istanbul.** A study of four chain hotels found physical adaptations generally present, but provisions for sensory impairments more limited. — [Ticaret Üniv.](https://acikerisim.ticaret.edu.tr/items/08d860ad-67da-4332-8fff-ccb765ae1c6b)
- **Staff and service.** A qualitative study of 36 disabled people in Istanbul found physical adaptations are not enough. Staff communication, paternalistic attitudes and missing accessibility knowledge leave disabled guests feeling secondary in the service process. — [DergiPark](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/5898543)
- **Cobblestones (official source).** Coverage of the Türkiye Erişilebilirlik Ödülleri notes that cobblestone squares in tourist areas were designed without wheelchair users in mind. — [Aile ve Sosyal Hizmetler Bakanlığı](https://www.aile.gov.tr/haberler/turkiye-erisilebilirlik-odulleri-sahiplerini-buldu/)
- **Families.** Hagia Sophia: children under 8 enter free with ID when accompanied. — [Orthodox Times](https://orthodoxtimes.com/new-entrance-fee-implemented-at-hagia-sophia-for-foreign-tourists/)
- **Accessible museums (promotional, single source).** Topkapı is promoted as wheelchair-suitable. The Beşiktaş JK Museum does not charge disabled visitors. — [istanbul.com](https://istanbul.com/tr/blog/general-info/accessible-istanbul)

### Inferences
- **Accessibility as structured, verified attributes.** Store step-free entrance, elevator, accessible toilet, slope or cobblestone severity, seating and shade, stroller-friendly. Record source and verification date for each. Where possible use OSM tags (wheelchair=*, toilets:wheelchair=*), and contribute verified data back to OSM.
- **Routing options:**
  - "avoid cobblestones and steep slopes" (relevant to Galata, Cihangir and Sultanahmet's hills);
  - "prefer tram/Marmaray with elevators";
  - "add rest stops every N minutes" for elderly travelers;
  - "family pace" with play areas and baby-care rooms.
- **Live elevator status.** Elevators break often, so the morning briefing could warn about outages at stations on the day's route, if Metro Istanbul publishes this data (unverified).

### Gaps
- No current data on elevator coverage or outages for Istanbul metro, Marmaray or other cities' transit, or whether there is an open data feed.
- No Turkey-specific research found on elderly travelers or families with young children (stroller access, kid-friendly dining, baby-care facilities).
- Outside Istanbul (Cappadocia cave hotels, Ephesus, Pamukkale travertines, coastal towns), accessibility is not documented in retrieved sources.
- Accessibility of sites run by the Ministry of Culture and Tourism (Kültür ve Turizm Bakanlığı) and of the MüzeKart system was not verified.
